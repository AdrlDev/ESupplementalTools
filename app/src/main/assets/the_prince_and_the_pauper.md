The Prince and the Pauper

(An excerpt) by Mark Twain

One January day, Tom Canty woke up hungry as usual. He wandered here and there in the city. He hardly noticed where he was going for his thoughts were busy with the splendor of his dreams. Soon, Tom was walking down a quiet, lovely road. The road led to a mighty and majestic place-Westminster. Tom stared in glad wonder at the magnificent array of English royalty. Perhaps, now, he would be able to see a prince of flesh and blood.

There were many country folks and people from the city. All at once, he caught sight of a spectacle that almost made him shout out of joy. Within was an attractive-looking boy. He was clothed in lovely silk and satin, shining with jewels. Several gorgeous gentlemen stood near him. Oh! He was a prince without a shadow of a doubt. The prayer of the pauper boy’s heart was answered at last.

Tom’s breath came quick and short with excitement. His eyes grew big with wonder and delight. He wanted to have a good, devouring look at the prince. The next instant, one of the soldiers snatched him rudely away.

The soldier said, “Mind your manners, you young beggar!”

The crowd jeered and [laughing] laughed. But the young prince sprang to the gate. He [crying] cried out, “[angrily] How dare you treat a poor boy that way! How dare you treat my father’s subject so! Open the gate and let the boy in!”

The crowd cheered and [shouts] shouted, “[cheering] Long live the Prince of Wales!”

The soldiers saluted, opened the gates, and saluted again. The little Prince of Poverty passed by in his fluttering rags. He joined hands with the Prince of Limitless Plenty, Edward Tudor.

Edward Tudor said, “You look tired and hungry. You have been treated badly. Come with me.” Edward took Tom to a rich apartment in the palace. Upon his command, a meal was brought in. The prince sent away his servants. Then he sat nearby and asked questions while Tom ate. The prince asked about Tom’s family, “Do your sisters forbid their servants to smile?””They? Oh, do you think, sir, that they have servants?”

The little prince then said, “And why not? Who helps them undress at night? Who helps them dress when they arise?”

“Their garment! Have they but one?”

“None, sir. Would you have them take off their garment and sleep without them like beasts?”

“Ah, your lordship, what would they do with more?”

“It is a quaint and marvelous thought! I beg your pardon, I had not meant to laugh. You speak well and have an easy grace. Do you go to school?”

“No, sir, but the kind priest, Father Andrew, taught me from his books.”

“Tell me of your Offal Court. Do you have a pleasant life there?”

“Yes, sir, except when one is hungry.”

“Tell me more.”

“We boys of Offal Court run races to see who the fastest of us is.”

“I’d like to do that, too.”

“In summer, sir, we wade and swim in the canals and in the river. We dive and shout and

Tumble and”

“It would be worth my father’s kingdom to enjoy it once! Please go on.”

“We dance and sing about the Maypole. We play in the sand. We wallow in the mud, sir.”

“Oh, it’s glorious! If I could only wear clothes like yours, and strip my feet, and revel in the mud once, just once.”

“And if I could only clothe me once, sweet sir, as you are clothed, just once-“

“Oh, would you like it? Then so shall it be. Take off your rags and wear my splendid clothes, boy! Let’s change before anybody comes.”

A few minutes later, the little Prince of Wales was garlanded with Tom’s fluttering odds and ends. The little Prince of Pauperdom was dressed in the gaudy plumage of royalty. The two went and stood side by side before a great mirror. A miracle! They stared at each other, then at the mirror, and then at each other again. At last the puzzled prince said. “What do you think of this?”

“Ah, your worship,” replied Tom. “Don’t make me answer. I am not worthy to say it.”

In a moment, he snatched up and put away an article of national importance that lay upon the table. Then he was out of the door. As soon as he reached the great gates, he seized the bars shouting, “Open! Unbar the gates!”

The soldier that had maltreated Tom obeyed promptly. As the prince burst through the gate, half-smothered with royal anger, the soldier gave him a sounding box on the ear, “Take that, you beggar boy, for what you got me from His Royal Highness!”

The crowd roared with [laughing] laughter. The prince picked himself out of the mud and [shouts] shouted fiercely to the soldier, “I am the Prince of Wales; my person is sacred and you shall hang for

Laying hands upon me!” The soldier saluted mockingly and said, “I salute your gracious Highness.” Then angrily,

“Be off, you crazy rubbish!”

Here, the jeering crowd closed around the poor little prince. They hustled him far down the road, hooting and shouting, “Make way for his Royal Highness! Make way for the Prince of Wales!”
